# Access-to-water-in-Rural-and-Urban-areas
The analysis of the dataset reveals significant insights into the trends of basic water access across global regions, with a specific focus on the disparities between urban and rural populations.

Key Takeaways
Persistent Urban-Rural Disparity: Across all analyzed regions, urban populations consistently enjoy higher levels of basic water access compared to rural populations. The most pronounced gap is observed in Sub-Saharan Africa, where urban access was approximately 85.7% in 2020, compared to only 54.8% for rural areas.

Positive Improvement Trends (2015–2020): Every region showed progress in expanding basic water access between 2015 and 2020. Notably, Middle East & North Africa and Sub-Saharan Africa recorded the highest rates of improvement in rural areas, with increases of 3.68% and 3.61% respectively.
Kenya Case Study: Kenya's data mirrors the broader regional trends in Sub-Saharan Africa. While urban access remained nearly stagnant at approximately 87%, rural basic water access saw a meaningful increase from 48.27% in 2015 to 51.78% in 2020, indicating targeted efforts in rural infrastructure.

Regional Variations in Access: Regions such as North America and Europe & Central Asia have achieved near-universal basic water access (above 96%) with minimal differences between urban and rural settings. In contrast, South Asia and East Asia & Pacific show moderate gaps of 6.5% and 11.6%, respectively.
Detailed Insights

Rural Improvement: The 3.5% increase in rural access over five years indicates positive movement in infrastructure development.

Urban Stability: Urban access in has reached a plateau, showing very little change (86.95% to 86.98%), suggesting that focus may need to shift toward maintaining quality or reaching the final 13% of the population.

High Disparity Regions: Beyond Sub-Saharan Africa, the Middle East & North Africa (9.5% gap) and East Asia & Pacific (11.6% gap) continue to struggle with equitable distribution.

Low Disparity Regions: North America (0.2% gap) and Europe & Central Asia (2.1% gap) serve as benchmarks for equitable water access.

Annual Rate of Change (ARC): Based on the regional averages, Sub-Saharan Africa has one of the highest Average Annual Rates of Change for national access (0.56%), reflecting the intensive efforts to bridge the existing gaps. Rural areas in Latin America & Caribbean showed a high ARC of 0.68%, indicating a strong regional focus on improving rural infrastructure.
